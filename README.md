# Cel Blade

Cel-shaded arcane-tech action MMORPG — Three.js, plain ES modules, no build step for the client;
a Node server runs the shared world.

```
npm install               # once (three, the WebSocket library and the Postgres driver, for the server)
npm start                 # game server: serves the client + the online realm at http://localhost:8080
ADMINS=youraccount npm start   # the same, with your account as an admin
npm test                  # power curve, horde, progression, place, settlement, story, dungeon, surface, building, raid, event and physics checks, then the smoke test: sign up, join the world, chat, form a raid group, go down a dungeon
npm run load [-- <url>]   # load tests (HTTP, sign-ins, players in the world) on localhost:8080, a throwaway server, or <url>
python serve.py 5173      # or: offline-only dev server (single-player), http://localhost:5173
```

How online play works (the shared world, combat netcode, parties, trade, guilds, mail) is in
[docs/MULTIPLAYER.md](docs/MULTIPLAYER.md). Running a realm (one process, Docker with TLS, or a Kubernetes cluster of gateways and layers that grows to thousands of players; settings, admins,
backups, monitoring, capacity) is in [docs/DEPLOY.md](docs/DEPLOY.md).

## Systems at a glance
- **Characters & animation** (`models/Characters.js`, `models/Human.js`, `anim/`): every character is a bone skeleton (hips → spine → chest → head, two-segment arms and legs with hands and feet) baked into skinned meshes (`ModelKit.bakeSkinned`: three per character whatever its colours — one lit mesh with each piece's colour in its vertices, one for the glowing parts, one ink shell with each piece's line weight in its vertices — indexed so the skinning shader transforms each shared vertex once; crowds stay cheap). Humans are built at real proportions — about seven heads tall, legs half the height — as one continuous lofted skin per region (torso and neck, each arm, each leg; `render/Loft.js`) with blended weights, so waist, ribs, neck, elbows, knees and hips bend smoothly; muscle shape (deltoids, biceps, forearms, glutes, quads, kneecaps, calves) is in the cross-sections, and clothes are bands of the same lofts, so they bend with the body. Hands are jointed fists — four three-segment fingers and a thumb that wrap the actual handle of whatever is held, or hang relaxed. Heads are sculpted: a cranium, a face lofted chin to brow (jaw, cheekbones), nose, brow, lips, ears, set-in eyes with lash lines and brows, hair caps to a real hairline (short spiky, long with locks, bun, bald) and full beards. `Animator.js` is fully procedural: a gait with planted feet (two-bone leg IK onto terrain), hip bob/sway, lean into acceleration, bank into turns, landing compression, flips, slides, dashes (a low lunge along the dash, weapon held steady) and mantles, all blended through springs; deaths are ragdolls (below). Weapons are held weapon-first (`WeaponMotion.js`): carries per weight class, guns placed against the body with both hands solved by IK, and melee swings that travel arcs around the torso. Weight shapes every swing: heavy weapons hang at the cocked point, keep accelerating through the hit, trail the hands then whip through, cut on a diagonal, and drag the body into a crouched, wide-stanced follow-through before heaving back; the whirlwind coils, builds speed with the blade at arm's length and carries past a full turn.
- **Collision and ragdolls** (`world/World.js`, `world/MeshCollision.js`, `anim/Ragdoll.js`): props and places collide with their real triangles — steep ones are walls at body height, up-facing ones floors, down-facing ones ceilings — so a ring of stones, a hut or a hollow stump can be walked round, stood on or dropped into, and a jump stops under a deck. Movement is swept in short steps and loses the speed going into whatever it hits, so nothing fast passes through a wall and a slide along one neither speeds up nor sticks. The dead fall as ragdolls of their own skeletons: balls at the joints and over the body's real outline (armour, shields, crests, robes), stiff torso, head and shin-and-foot pieces, joints limited the way they bend, kicked the way the blow came; they land on the terrain, props and dungeon floors, stay their side of walls, and come to rest within a few seconds. Bones only turn, so nothing stretches. A limb or head shot off (`entities/Dismemberment.js`) takes copies of its bones with it and flies as a ragdoll of its own — an arm folding at the elbow and wrist, a beast's leg at the knee — before it lands, lies still and fades.
- **Guns** (`combat/Projectiles.js`): ballistic rounds at real muzzle velocities (360–920 m/s) with drop, swept ray hits (no tunnelling), thin tracers (every third round bright), surface-specific impacts with bullet holes, ejected casings and camera recoil. Spread is screen-space: the reticle keeps its size and aiming down sights tightens the cone through the zoom.
- **Holding weapons** (`anim/WeaponMotion.js`): pistols are one-handed; long guns rest at low ready (stock seated, muzzle dipped, sights up), seat the stock in the shoulder to aim (torso bladed, support hand on a real foregrip); launchers ride the shoulder. Blades, hafts and staves run *through* the fist, thumb toward the head, and each hand turns about the handle so its wrist faces the arm (`Animator.js` `handleFrame`). Melee weapons are gripped low on the handle and swing on arcs split between torso turn and arm sweep, so blades and pommels stay clear of the body; windups travel along the arc.
- **Bodies, armour and capes** (`render/Shell.js`, `models/`): chest armour is lofted from the body's own torso (`Human.BODY`, superellipse shells with pecs and shoulder blades), so plates hug it; helms are fitted to the head and leg armour stretched to the legs (`ArmorForms.applyArmor`). Five armour slots — head, chest, hands, legs and **back**: capes are skinned cloth on a grid of five bone chains simulated by `anim/Cloth.js` (gravity, air drag and internal damping, flutter waves, folding across the width, bending, collision with the back's real curve, seat, legs and shoulder line), so they ripple side to side as well as swing.
- **First person** (V; `render/CameraRig.js`, `render/ViewModel.js`): the camera rides a steady eye height that follows how low the body is carried (crouches, slides, landing dips and climbs, all eased) but never the head's own motion — flips, corkscrews, lunges and gait bob don't move the view; the body is drawn shadow-only and faces where you look. The held weapon is a viewmodel with the body's own jointed hands (fingers round the handle, the pistol grip or the foregrip; the free hand relaxed) and bare forearms in the character's skin tone — both hands on two-handed weapons and gun foregrips — in a hold per type (daggers forward, swords and axes up at the right, greatswords and hammers across the body, spears couched, staves upright). Melee swings follow each attack's path and timing: an anticipation pull, an accelerating strike that crosses the view, a follow-through, a blade trail, a camera roll into the stroke and a jolt on impact. Guns raise their real sights to the screen centre when aiming — a notched rear sight and glowing front post, a holo sight whose dot is collimated (it always marks where the gun points), or a full scope view with extra zoom — and aimed shots follow the sight line, so they land on the dot even as recoil and sway move it. Aimed recoil is mostly a push straight back, so automatic fire keeps the sight in view. Every sight is styled by maker (`items/WeaponModel.js`): scopes on the model and in the scope view (`ui/ScopeView.js`) — Kessler military mil-dots with a rangefinder, Rattlecap scratched brass with a cracked lens, Voltaic digital chevron with a live readout and scanlines, Rime crystal with a frosted rim; holo sights — Kessler's closed box hood with a ring-and-ticks reticle, Rattlecap's lens wired into a brass ring with a painted cross, Voltaic's white loop with a glowing chevron, Rime's hexagonal crystal frame with a diamond; iron sights — Kessler's peep and winged post, Rattlecap's bent-plate V-notch and bolt post, Voltaic's glowing three-dot sights, Rime's crystal-shard notch and ice-spike post (raised on a riser wherever the receiver would block the line). Crossbows carry theirs above the bolt; launchers on a bracket along the tube's left side, so aiming puts the tube on your right shoulder. Ink outlines on the first-person weapon and hands are drawn on their own thinner layer, which thins further as you aim, so they never swallow a sight. Also walk bob, look sway, reload, sprint and swap poses; shots, muzzle flash and casings come from the viewmodel's gun.
- **Hitstop**: a landed blow freezes only the two fighters for a beat; the rest of the world (effects, other enemies, shots) keeps moving. Loot from a kill is built a few frames later so it never lands on the killing blow's frame.
- **Codex** (tome): burns a rune where you aim that detonates a moment later; charged casts ripple five runes down the aim line.
- **Graphics quality** (Settings › Graphics quality, `render/Quality.js`; phones start on Low, everything else on High):
  - *Antialiasing* (Settings › Antialiasing, `render/PostFX.js`): Off, FXAA on the finished image, 4× MSAA on geometry, or both. FXAA also smooths the screen-space ink outlines; off by default on phones and tablets.
  - *Cel shading* (`render/Toon.js`, every quality): four light bands (shadow, turned away, turned toward, lit) with edges softened over a pixel on curved surfaces; shadows lean cool and violet, as in painted cel art, and the shadow band is hatched in screen-space ink; characters and enemies get a thin rim of sunlight on the edges that face it, so they read against busy ground.
  - *Procedural surfaces* (Medium and High, `render/Surfaces.js`): planks, stone blocks, brick, riveted metal panels, corrugated sheet, roof tiles, shingles, thatch, plaster, adobe, fabric, rust, concrete, cobbles, rock, resin and carved stone, drawn in the shader with no textures — crisp seams, a tint per board, block or tile, a little grain. Every piece a world builder bakes carries coordinates in metres along its own faces and its kind (named by the builder, or by its palette colour: wood is planks, stone is blocks, metal is panels…), so patterns follow each wall and tile at true scale whichever way it's turned, even merged into one mesh; they fade to their average as they shrink toward a pixel. Dungeons are drawn in their theme's (concrete and rust, resin, panels, carved stone, rock). The ground has its own: painted two-tone patches (warmer on grass), pebbles on sand and dirt, cracks across steep rock.
  - *Buildings* (`world/build/`, every quality): a modular, procedural kit builds every house, shop, inn, workshop, Warden post and citadel in the towns, cities and Brightwater, the hamlets' and villages' cottages, barns and huts, the Scav camps' shacks and the strongholds' towers. Each is a seeded plan — a block, an L or a T; gable, hip, shed, parapet, crenellated, sawtooth or domed roofs; windows in bays on every face and storey — dressed by style: half-timbering, shutters and flower boxes in green country, adobe, arches and beam ends in the dry lands, thatch and porches in the wet, steel and pipes against the cold, white panels and light strips in the strange lands, quoins and hipped tile in the cities, crenellations for the Wardens, welded scrap and spikes for the Scavs. The server's world plans the same buildings for their colliders without building them.
  - *Screen-space ink* (`render/PostFX.js`): outlines drawn from a depth and normal pre-pass — silhouettes and creases sharper than ~60° on everything, fading with the fog. They replace the world's inverted-hull outlines; the first-person weapon keeps its own thinner hull ink and is masked out.
  - *Bloom*: glowing things (every unlit material: energy, crystals, runes, shots, glowing blades) are lifted past white so only they bloom, then brought back into range keeping their hue.
  - *Ambient occlusion* (High): from the same pre-pass, at half resolution — creases, contact under feet, rocks set into the ground.
  - *Cascaded shadows* (High, `render/Shadows.js`): two cascades out to 150 m, so distant trees and buildings cast shadows. Lit materials are set up the first time they appear in the scene, and put back when cascades are switched off.
  - *Grass* (Medium and High, `render/Grass.js`): a field of up to 70,000 blades round the player in one instanced draw, fixed in the world (it doesn't swim as you walk), coloured like the ground beneath and thinning on roads, rock, sand, snow and dirt yards; it bends with the wind and away from your feet.
  - *Wind* (`render/Wind.js`, every quality): trees, bushes, reeds, grass tufts and flowers sway downwind, crowns more than trunks, with gusts rolling across the land — their outlines, shadows and ink sway with them.
  - *Speed FX*: zoom blur toward your direction of travel plus comic speed lines, scaled by speed (off when slow).
  - On Low the scene is drawn straight to the screen with hull ink and flat-coloured surfaces, going through the post chain only for the speed effect.
- **Endless world** (`src/world/`): generated from the save's seed (`Gen.js`) — continental noise, mountain ranges, rivers and lakes, hills that stand up into ridgelines in rugged country, and twelve climate-driven biomes, each with its own ground, vegetation, sky and fog: meadow, forest, highland steppe, snow, marsh, ash wastes, mountains, **desert** (dune seas with long windward slopes and slip faces, cacti, mesas), **badlands** (plateaus stepped into banded red-rock terraces and cut by winding canyons, hoodoos), alien **jungle** (palms and giant ferns), **crystal fields** (glowing veins, crystal clusters and spires) and **fungal groves** (spore moss, giant mushrooms). The land is cut into named Voronoi regions (~240 m), each owned by a faction and levelled by its distance from the hub, with its own waystone, camps (and at higher levels a stronghold with a boss), towers, ruins, mesas, chests and roads linking the waystones.
  - *Landmarks* (`Landmarks.js`, about one region in three of four): one-off sights picked to suit the biome, each named on the map with a cache to find — a starship wreck nose-down in its furrow, a colossal skeleton, a precursor ring gate, a kneeling titan mech, a monolith circle, crystal spires, an elder tree, giant mushroom caps, a deep-space dish array, a ruined arcology, a red-rock arch, a fallen statue (a carved head on its side, a hand raised from the ground), volcanic vents and the stump of a space elevator.
  - *Settlements of every size* (`places.js` lays them out with the generator, `Towns.js` builds them, `settlements.js` dresses their folk), all safe havens roaming packs keep out of:
    - **Hamlets**, off the roads in about two regions in five: a farm's cottages, a prospector's claim, reed huts on stilts or a nomad camp — two or three folk round a fire, one of them with an errand for you.
    - **Villages** at about a third of the waystones, so the roads run through them: homesteads (farmhouse, barn, silo, wind turbine, crops), frontier outposts (prefab walls, gate turrets, watchtower, habs, landing pad), mining camps (drill rig over a glowing seam, conveyor, crane), desert caravans (crawler wagons under bright canopies) and fen villages on stilts — each with a trader and a quest board.
    - **Towns** at about one waystone in nine (one per region and the ring round it), walled, with gates where the roads come in: a market (a gunsmith and an armourer, who also carry a fine stock — rare or better, a level ahead), a quest board, the inn (rest there and you wake there if you fall), the workshop (its smith resets your talents) and the Warden post (contracts), houses round the ring street, 8–12 townsfolk, and the Wardens' waygate on the plaza (the way down into the dungeons, as a private run).
    - **The four story cities**, one at the heart of each act's lands — New Meridian about 500 m out, then Farcall, Ironreach and Anchorage — on the map from the start. A walled heart round the waystone (the Warden citadel, a grand inn, a quest board and the war table, which marks the strongholds round the city) and four districts: the market (gunsmith, armourer, merchant), the crafts quarter (the master smith), the homes, and the gate yard, with the Wardens' dungeon waygate and the raid gate.
    - **Brightwater** is a town now: the old camp is its plaza (the reactor is its inn), ringed by a workshop (with a waygate before it), a gunsmith's, an armourer's, a quest board, houses and walls.
    - Folk are built as you come near (nearest first), animate fully up close and a few times a second further off, and fade out with distance; each town's or district's buildings are merged by colour (a handful of draw calls), and a city streams in a part at a time (heart, walls, four districts). The server finds each of the folk by id and checks you stand with them — to buy, rest, reset talents, or take and hand in a quest.
  - `Chunks.js` streams 64 m terrain chunks around the player with three LODs; `World.js` streams points of interest, roads, instanced scenery and colliders in and out with them, within a per-frame time budget.
- **Enemy factions** (`combat/bestiary.js`, `combat/races.js`; models in `models/Scavs.js`, `Hive.js`, `Synths.js` and `Fauna.js`, registered in `models/Creatures.js`): four factions — humanoid, alien, robotic and wild — each with its own mob types, fighting styles and level-gated sub-types; sub-types in brackets —
  - *The Scav clans* (human raiders on the human body, in salvaged armour): blade-swarming cutters (rippers), riot-shielded breachers whose guard blocks frontal hits (ironclads), gas-masked grenadiers (firebombers), techs who throw shields round their allies, repair them and zap you (arc techs), exo-suited juggernauts, and the Warlord in a crimson exo-suit under trophy banners.
  - *The Hive* (alien insectoids — ant-waisted, antennaed, compound-eyed): scythe-armed drones (bloaters, whose acid sacs burst when they die), spitters with a living cannon for a forearm (lancers firing piercing spines), floating psions that blink away and cast homing psi orbs (cryo psions), shielded praetorians with bone sword-arms (champions), and the Queen with her crown of chitin and trailing egg sac.
  - *The Synths* (white-shelled war machines): sentry drones that orbit overhead and fire bursts (arc sentries), monoblade stalkers that charge, mortar-backed bulwarks (siege), and the Colossus.
  - *The Wild* (this world's animals): six-legged razorhound packs that circle and dart in (rimehounds, alphas whose howl frenzies the pack), scorpion-tailed crawlers that spit resin and pounce from ambush (venom), armour-shelled thunderhorns that charge (ironhides), and the crystal-grown Behemoth.
  Bipeds share the humanoid animator; the wild's animals run on `anim/LegAnimator.js` (per-leg gait cycles — trots, tripods, eight-legged scuttles — with IK foot planting, the body riding the terrain, bite/charge/pounce/rear/howl poses) and sentries and psions on its hover rig. Every attack is data (`Enemy.js` runs swing, combo, bash, bite, charge, pounce, arrow, bolt, burst, fan, lob, stomp, nova, ward, heal, howl and summon), and so is how a type looks doing it: arm stance, stoop, and where its shots leave. Enemy hits carry their element's status. Camps are themed to match: scav junk camps and salvage yards, hive nests (creep veined with light, egg clutches, chitin spires), synth relay nodes, and the wild's dens; each faction's stronghold has its own walls.
- **Encounters** (`combat/races.js`, `sim/WorldSim.js`): camps spawn near the player and individual enemies respawn after a delay. Camps grow into hordes with their region's level — 4–6 at levels 1–4, 6–9 with the odd elite at 5–9, 8–12 led by one or two elites (and in 30% of camps a **champion**) at 10–17, 10–16 with two or three elites and always a champion from 18. Roaming packs belong to the faction whose land they cross; in regions of level 10 and up some are whole **warbands** of 10–20 on the march, announced and ringed on the minimap, with a bonus (XP, gold and a rare-or-better piece) for whoever breaks one. **Strongholds** hold their boss and 10–14 guards; attacking one raises the alarm, and reinforcements march in from the far side in waves while the boss stands; storming it pays a bonus too. Enemies take turns through attack tokens per player, more of them as the crowd on you grows, and those waiting work round to your flanks and back. Bosses call reinforcements as they weaken. Resting (at Brightwater's reactor or an inn) resets camps and chests.
- **Elites and affixes** (`sim/affixes.js`): named elites (over three times the health, a little over twice the bite) carry an affix — champions two, and one more each from level 18 — shown as an aura at their feet and named on their nameplate: *Shielded* (a shield of half its health to break first, back after a lull), *Frenzied* (faster and fiercer below half health), *Vampiric* (regenerates while fighting), *Volatile* (explodes after it dies), *Blinking* (teleports to you), *Splitting* (Hive: comes apart in two), *Overcharged* (Synths: shock pulses round it) and *Commander* (allies near it hit harder and take less).
- **Health**: there's no passive healing in a fight. Out of one (five seconds unhurt) health comes back quickly; **health cells** drop from some of the dead (always from elites and bosses, bigger) and drift to you when you're hurt and close; level-ups and resting heal fully. Cells are each player's own (health is each screen's business online).
- **Dungeons** (`world/dungeons.js`, `world/DungeonGen.js`, `sim/Dungeons.js`): the Rustjaw Foundry (Ironjaw's fortress, Act I), the Sunken Vault (behind the seal the story breaks) and the Brood Pits (Hive tunnels out past Farcall).
  - *Two ways down*: the Foundry and the Brood Pits have an open-world copy, shared by everyone on the layer, its rooms refilling a few minutes after they're emptied. Any of them (the Vault only this way) can be run as a **private copy** for your party at a tier — Normal, Veteran or Elite, the run's Threat for everyone in it, at least your own level — where nothing comes back once it's beaten and, past the final boss, a **reward chest** opens once a day per dungeon per tier. Go down at the dungeon's own way in (the Foundry's furnace hall gate, the Vault's door, the Pits' mouth) or through the Wardens' waygate in any town (Brightwater too) or city's gate yard.
  - *Layouts*: each dungeon's main route is fixed by its seed — an entrance hall, three halls, the mini-boss's arena, two halls with a lift down between them, the final boss's hall — behind a locked door (its key in a side room), a door that needs power (nodes switched on in two side rooms) and the arena's seal (open once the mini-boss falls); stairs where it drops a level. Side rooms, who lives where, traps and treasure are reshuffled for every private run. Traps: vents that glow then burst, turrets that track you, spore pods that swell and burst. Five themes are built: Scav bunkers, Hive tunnels, Synth facilities, Maker vaults and Wild caverns (the last two in the dev panel for now).
  - *Down there* (`world/Interior.js`, `world/InteriorKit.js`, `world/DungeonView.js`): each copy is built in a pocket far out past the land, so its floors are the world's ground there and its walls and doors are box colliders — movement, physics, shots, the camera and enemies all work as outside. Enemies find their way round walls and through doors on the copy's grid (`sim/Nav.js`) and can't see you through walls. The land, the sky and the grass go; the fog closes in and the light comes from the theme's fixtures. A loading screen on the way in and out; the minimap and the map show the rooms you've seen, and the compass points at what the dungeon asks of you next (the key, the power, the arena, the boss, the way out). Fall and you wake in its entrance hall; leave by the portal there, or the one past the boss; a game saved down there comes back outside.
- **Raids** (`world/raids.js`, `sim/RaidRun.js`, `sim/BossScript.js`, `combat/bosses.js`; gameplay phase 7): instances for a **raid group** — up to four parties linked, twelve players (`server/raidgroups.js`: `/raid form` or a city's war table, `/raid invite Name`, `/ra` to talk, raid frames on the HUD) — or a lone party or player, entered at a city's raid gate from level 14, at Normal, Veteran or Elite.
  - *The Brood Mother*: halls of Hive trash between three arenas, each with a gate that shuts behind the raid when its boss is pulled (a wipe resets the boss and opens it again; a kill opens the seal onward), and a chest of its own, once a week per tier. **The Brood Warden** (a guard that turns frontal blows, a sweeping cone, acid spat at the spread-out, a rush down a marked line, the brood out of the walls, a carapace to break before it bursts); **the Psion Choir** (three psions — a ring of dirge, beams of shriek, a lullaby the raid must share — and seams strung between them; each one down makes the others louder); **the Brood Mother** (egg clutches that hatch if left, three Brood Hearts that make her immune until they're torn out, spore rain, a charge at whoever's furthest, a gravid burst to share). Each enrages after a few minutes.
  - *Boss scripts* are data: phases at health thresholds with their own attacks, mechanics on timers — shapes marked on the floor that land (circles, rings, cones, lines, crosses; on the boss, on players, following one, scattered round the arena), pools left burning, soaks, adds, shields, immunity while adds live, charges down marked lines — and an enrage timer. Boss health and how many of each mechanic come follow how many are in the fight. The marks are drawn and judged on each screen (`fx/Telegraphs.js`), like enemy shots; the server counts who shared a soak. The HUD shows the encounter: each boss's health and shield, its phase, and the time to its enrage.
- **Public events** (`sim/WorldEvents.js`, `ui/EventUI.js`, `world/EventProps.js`; gameplay phase 6a): a director runs inside the world sim (one per layer online, one offline) and, every few minutes, starts an event 120–320 m from a player who's been about a while — never in the starting lands, the hub or a city, never two within 600 m. Each is announced (banner, chat, a ring on the minimap and the map, a diamond on the compass), gathers for 40 seconds round its beacon — press E there to vote the tier (Normal, Veteran, Elite; the most votes win, ties go lower) — then runs at that tier, which is everyone's Threat inside it.
  - *Hive Surge* (a settlement in Hive land, or anywhere from level 8 now and then): three Warden pylons round its heart, spore pods rooted outside hatching drones until they're burst, four waves. *Scav Siege* (a settlement in Scav land): siege engines with their crews shell the pylons from 40 m; break them, then hold off two waves, the last led by a Siege Captain. *Maker Anomaly* (a landmark or ruin): the beacon charges while someone stands by it and stalls while a guardian does; Synth waves pour from the rift beside it, a Maker Warden at half charge.
  - *Taking part*: anyone in the event's area. The attackers go for the pylons or the beacon (their health is over them and on the tracker) unless someone hurts them enough to draw them off; waves, pods, engines and the things to defend all scale with how many are there. A point a second there and 100 per enemy's worth of damage dealt make your part; 60 earns the event chest (XP, gold, two rare-or-better items, a third for anyone with half the top score), handed out at the beacon.
  - *Losing*: a settlement whose pylons all fall (or that nobody comes to defend) is overrun for 15 minutes — its folk gone, its traders, inn, board and quest givers shut (the server refuses them) — and a *Retake* begins at once: clear the garrison holding it to free it and earn the chest. Left alone, the garrison moves on after the 15 minutes.
  - The tracker under the minimap shows the event nearest you (or the one you're in): its step, the vote, waves, pods or engines left, the beacon's charge, each pylon's health, your part. Spore Pods and Siege Engines are new enemies (`models/EventFoes.js`) with codex entries.
- **Map & discovery**: fog of war (`Discovery.js`, sparse per-chunk cells), full map (`ui/MapUI.js`) with region names, faction colours, hamlets, villages, towns and the cities, the story's places (in gold), public events, custom markers and waystone fast travel; minimap and compass show the tracked quest's marker, the public event near you and custom markers.
- **Audio** (`audio/`): all procedural Web Audio — guns voiced per type (crack, body, tail, action) and tinted by element and maker, reloads timed to the animation, weighted melee whooshes and hits, spell casts, explosions, footsteps by surface, movement sounds, wind/slide/glide beds, and enemy calls (howls, roars, psi casts, acid spits, shield clangs, fuses). Volume and mute live in Settings.
- **Loot**: weapons (18 types) and armour (5 slots) are generated from recipes — type → rarity → maker → stat parts → visual component forms → perks. Saves store only recipes.
  - *Makers* (`items/manufacturers.js`) are the world's sources of kit, each with its own weapon line, stat bias, element, palette and signature energy colour, emblem, component shapes (`items/forms.js`), scope style and gun voice: **Frontier Outfitters** (settler rifles, crossbows, spears), the **Warden Arsenal** (military rifles, snipers, launchers, swords), **Ironhide Heavy Industries** (mining hammers, axes, shotguns), **Rustjaw Salvage** (scav-built SMGs, shotguns and launchers; fire), **Hivecraft** (bio-weapons grown from Hive chitin; corrosive), **Voltaic Synthworks** (rewired synth tech; shock) and **Maker Relics** (precursor staves, codices and orbs; frost).
  - *Armour sets* (`items/sets.js`): every armour piece belongs to its maker's set, which fixes its look (`models/ArmorForms.js`, in the maker's colours) and name, so the pieces of a set match — **Pathfinder** (frontier hat, rig, trail boots, poncho), **Vanguard** (Warden power armour), **Bastion** (mining helm, plate, power fists, power pack), **Raider** (rebreather mask, scrap plates, wraps, shroud), **Chitin** (compound-eyed crest, carapace, talons, wing-cases), **Arcframe** (synth faceplate, reactor core, emitters) and **Ascendant** (crown, vestment, sigils, halo) — and **Broodbane**, heavy Hive chitin gilded where it cracked, which only the Brood Mother's raid gives (always legendary, in colours of its own). Wearing two and four pieces of a set grants its bonuses (`rpg/Stats.js`) — class or element damage, resists, mobility, and a set effect (lifesteal, kills that detonate, lightning on crits, knockback immunity…); item cards and the Character tab show pieces worn and which bonuses are live.
  - *Themed drops*: each faction's dead drop mostly their own kit (scavs Rustjaw and Ironhide, the Hive Hivecraft, synths Voltaic and Warden gear, the wild Frontier), caches hold what suits where they sit (Maker relics at landmarks and ruins, Warden kit on sensor masts), and quest givers reward their own (the Warden's arsenal, the sage's relics).
- **Weapon names** (`items/names.js`): the base name is a model line taken from the frame the weapon is built around — a gun's receiver, a blade's hilt, a haft, a shaft, a launcher tube, a crossbow's prod, a codex cover, an orb shell — plus its type, so guns on the same receiver share a line ("Talon Rifle", "Talon Pistol"). One supplementing word comes from what's fitted: the elemental core if there is one ("Caustic Talon Rifle"), otherwise the most notable attachment ("Sniping Hauler Rifle"). Legendaries are named only after their legendary power and class ("Emberfang", "The Ravenous Verdict", "Volt's Verdict"), with no model line or supplement.
- **RPG**: levels (to 30), talents and abilities, gear stats, quests (`rpg/Quests.js`), traders, backpack, loot filter, achievements and titles, the codex.
  - *Quests* (`rpg/Quests.js`): quests run in stages, each with its objectives — go somewhere, talk to someone, defeat a type, a faction or a named foe, clear a camp, use an object, collect quest drops, hold a place against waves, answer a question — and story flags remember what happened and what you chose, so what people say follows it.
    - *The story* (`rpg/storyline.js`): the Prologue (*The Last Sleeper*, levels 1–3) and Act I (*Signal Fire*, 3–8), twelve quests from Warden Maren and Old Ysolde at Brightwater to High Warden Ashe in New Meridian — the wreck of the Pilgrim, a raided village, the Wardens' relic dig, the signal beacons, the Rustjaw Outpost and Foundry (where Ironjaw waits, and his lieutenant Krell's fate is yours to decide), and the Sunken Vault he was digging out. Its places stand in every world like the cities (`world/story.js`). Short moments (`ui/Moments.js`: a few camera shots and lines under a letterbox, a title card for the big ones; skippable) mark the turns, and folk call out as you arrive.
    - *Side quests*: each kind of village has a three-part story of its own (`rpg/sidequests.js`: a homestead's missing child, a mine's cave-in, a caravan's long road, an outpost's lost signal, a fen village's lanterns). Generated work runs alongside: every town's and city's Warden gives contracts (clear a named camp near your level; every fourth, a stronghold), hamlet folk give errands (the camp raiding them, a pack to thin out, supplies lost in the wilds), and quest boards (villages, towns, cities) show three postings at a time — a bounty on the local faction, a camp to clear (sometimes with captives to free), a salvage run, a survey of the waystones or a delivery.
    - *Journal and tracker*: the journal (L) sorts quests into Story, Side and Contracts, each at its step with what's left and who it goes back to, and shows the story so far, act by act. Track a quest and it leads the HUD's tracker, the compass, the minimap and the map. Over people's heads a gold marker means a quest to take, green one to hand in, and teal someone a quest wants a word with.
  - *Talents* (`rpg/talents.js`, the Talents tab, N): a point a level from level 2 — 29 at the cap — in three trees: **Vanguard** (melee and toughness), **Gunslinger** (guns and mobility) and **Technomancer** (magic, tech and support). Each tree's five tiers open at 0, 3, 6, 10 and 15 points spent in it, and hold bonuses of up to three ranks (class damage, armour, health, crit, speed, mana…), an ability to unlock, upgrades for the tree's abilities, and a capstone. A tree takes 24 points to fill, so you fill one and dip into another; a full tree is about +60% to its class. Resetting is free below level 10, then costs gold, from a smith at a town's or city's workshop. (Talents replaced attributes: older characters get every point back to spend.) Armour's old attribute affixes are class damage and health percentages now.
  - *Abilities* (`combat/abilities.js`, `entities/AbilityKit.js`): twelve, four per tree, on X, G and T (three buttons over the action cluster on touch), each with its own cooldown on the bar over the XP line. **Vanguard**: Seismic Slam (a blast that knocks enemies down), Bulwark (a frontal shield that stops shots), Leap Strike (leap to the reticle and land in a blast), Rally Cry (heals and strengthens your party). **Gunslinger**: Frag Grenade, Grapple Line (pull yourself to what you aim at), Holo-Decoy (enemies near it go for it instead), Overclock (fire, reload, swing and cast half as fast again). **Technomancer**: Sentry Drone (a turret), Stasis Field (slows everything in it), Arc Barrier (a dome that stops enemy shots), Restoration Pulse (heals your party over time). Each has an upgrade further down its tree. Their damage scales with your level and class bonus rather than the weapon in hand, and runs through the usual damage pipeline (so it's reported like any hit); the server checks each use (unlocked, slotted, off cooldown) and shows it to everyone near — a party member's Rally and Pulse reach you, and their Arc Barrier stops shots on your screen too.
  - *Achievements and titles* (`rpg/achievements.js`, Journal › Achievements): 73, with points, across the story (the Prologue, the beacons, Ironjaw, Krell's fate, Act I, the villages' own stories), dungeons (a final boss, all three, private runs, an Elite run), raids (each of the Brood Mother's bosses, Veteran and Elite clears, a boss down with nobody falling, the full Broodbane set), public events (one, 25, each kind, one on Elite, a retake, leading one of three or more), combat (by faction, elites and affixes, every boss, warbands, strongholds, kills on harder Threat), exploration (waystones, landmarks, every biome, settlements, hamlets, towns, the four cities), collections (legendaries, full sets, every maker), progression and feats (a 50-hit combo, five kills in two seconds, a kill from the air). They count from the character's records, which change only through the character's operations, so the server counts them online. Twenty-eight grant a title to wear (Character tab), shown under your name and in chat.
  - *Codex* (Journal › Codex, `rpg/codex.js`): an entry per enemy type, opened by its first kill — lore, strengths and weaknesses, how many you've defeated, the sub-types and elite affixes you've seen — and entries for each kind of landmark you've found and each maker whose gear you've carried.
  - *The power curve* (`rpg/balance.js`): weapons grow 16% a level and enemy health 17% (talents make up the difference), player health 13% and enemy damage a touch more (talents add toughness), and armour stops a share of a hit measured against the attacker's level — so a fight against enemies of your own level stays about as hard at level 30 as at 1: trash dies in 3–4 hits and kills you in 12–16, elites take 10–15 and kill you in 5–7, a boss takes 60–90 seconds alone. Enemies of levels 1–4 are gentler (they reach full strength at 5), and new characters start in the Pathfinder trail set. Rarity is a smaller step than it was (common ×1.0 to legendary ×1.3) and makers and parts lean ±10%, so best-in-slot gear kills about 1.5–1.8× faster than on-level rare gear, never in one hit. Enemies three or more levels above you shrug off some of your damage; anything five or more below gives a tenth of the XP and no extra loot luck. `node tools/powercurve.mjs` prints the curve level by level (for a Vanguard spending talents down their tree, and a Gunslinger's toughness) and fails if any level leaves its band.
  - *Threat* (Character tab): Normal, Veteran or Elite — enemies hit you harder (×1.45, ×1.85) and take less from you (÷1.6, ÷2.5), and your kills give more XP (+25%, +50%), more luck and a rarity floor (uncommon, rare). Enemies are shared, so it only changes the fight for you; it can't change mid-fight. In a dungeon's or a raid's private run its tier is the Threat for everyone in it.
- **Title screen** (`ui/TitleMenu.js`): the logo and menu over a slow flight round your character — Continue (with the save's level, region and gold), New Game (confirmed before it replaces a save), Settings and Controls; arrow keys or W/S and Enter work too.
- **Game menu & settings** (`ui/GameMenu.js`, `core/Prefs.js`): a full-screen hub with tabs — Character (level, abilities on hand, title, combat stats, equipped gear, sets, Threat, loot filter), Talents (`ui/TalentsUI.js`), Inventory (`ui/InventoryUI.js`; also a trader's shop — their own stock, and a gunsmith's or armourer's fine stock), Journal (quests, achievements, codex: `ui/JournalUI.js`), Map (`ui/MapUI.js`), and online Social (party, players nearby, friends), Guild and Mail (`ui/SocialUI.js`), then Settings and Controls. It opens on Esc or whenever play loses the mouse, and it doesn't stop the world: the character stands idle (and can still be hit — the header shows live health). Only single-player freezes, and only while the window is in the background. Settings apply live, are stored per device rather than in the save, and are reachable from the title screen. Browsers only hand the mouse back after a click, so returning to play by keyboard shows a "click to resume" prompt.
- **Phones and tablets** (`ui/TouchControls.js`, `core/Device.js`): touch controls drive the same input state as keyboard and mouse (`core/Input.js`): a floating stick, drag-to-look, an action cluster under the right thumb, and the HUD's own prompts as buttons. The HUD rearranges round the thumbs, menus scale down on short screens, and portrait asks to be turned. Phones start on lighter graphics (antialiasing, shadows, hatching and speed effects off; 75% resolution), and adaptive resolution steps the resolution down while frames run slow. A web app manifest lets it be installed and run fullscreen in landscape.
- **Online** (`server/`, `net/`, `sim/`, `ui/TitleMenu.js`): when the page comes from the game server, the title signs you in (accounts, up to six characters each, a look picker) and enters the shared realm. The server runs the world's enemies (`sim/`, the same code offline single-player runs locally) and owns every character (`rpg/CharacterCore.js`): the client predicts and the server confirms. Other players appear fully animated in their own gear. Kills share XP and gold with everyone who fought, and each player gets their own loot roll. Parties of four (`ui/PartyUI.js`), a two-sided trade window (`ui/TradeUI.js`), friends, guilds with tags and chat, and mail with attachments (`ui/SocialUI.js`); world, nearby, whisper, party and guild chat (`ui/ChatUI.js`). In the cluster the realm runs as layers (copies of the world, ~110 players each) behind gateways (`server/gateway.js`), sharing Postgres; chat, friends, guilds, mail and admin reach across them and parties are brought together on one. See [docs/MULTIPLAYER.md](docs/MULTIPLAYER.md).
- **HUD** (`ui/HUD.js`, `style.css`): frontier sci-fi interface — chamfered dark-glass panels with hairline edges, energy-teal and gilt accents, diamond markers and tracked condensed capitals (Saira Condensed + Chakra Petch). Compass strip, slanted segmented vitals with a hexagonal level badge, weapon card with magazine pips, damage-direction arcs, low-health vignette.
- **Ink**: on Low (and on the first-person weapon at every quality) outlines are inverted hulls; their width is set per layer (`INK_SCALE` in `render/Toon.js`). World outlines thin as the view zooms in (aiming, scopes) so a magnified target isn't buried in ink, and the first-person weapon has its own thinner layer that thins again while aiming.
